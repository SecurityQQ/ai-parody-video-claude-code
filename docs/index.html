What is this?

"Not you." is a 40.8-second parody video at 854×480. A film director re-casts Nvidia's keynote opener on camera: he stops Jensen Huang in the first ten seconds, takes the leather jacket and hands the show to Tim Cook, who presents a graphics card that folds. It re-stages Apple's September 2026 keynote opener with the handoff flipped.

An AI agent made it on 2026-11-11 from one request: Claude Code running Claude Fable 5.2, with ten open-source varg video skills and the image, voice, music and video models behind one varg account. The repo is the project as the agent left it, cleaned for publishing: references, moodboard, script, sound plan, character sheets, 3D blockout, shot prompts, the edit as a HyperFrames project, and the judge's measured reports.

The request

The whole brief, as typed:

make a video where tim cook is replacing jensen huang because of the model incident. take their pictures and make a funny video with it; make it 480p please

use ten steps [one guide per step: references, moodboards, sounds, video scripts, characters, Blender/three.js, video generation, HyperFrames, AI judge, GitHub]

Mostly just take another funny video and adjust it.

The agent read "another funny video" as Apple's keynote opener, in which Tim Cook says "No, no, no, no, no. Not me. That's your guy." and hands the opening to someone else. Here the director says "Not you." Its full reading of the request, and the limits it set for itself, are in brief.md. A prompt you can paste to make your own is in PROMPT.md.

The 10 steps

Each step has a folder in the repo with a short README, the working files and the skill that ran it. Sound is locked before any picture is made, and the picture is cut to it.

  1. 01

    References

    Which existing video do we adjust, and what exactly does it do?

    23 logged searches, most of them over X, Wikimedia Commons, The Met and the Library of Congress. Picked Apple's opener and measured it: cuts at 3.14, 6.24 and 16.82 s, palette, word times. Read which jokes people had already liked

    Skill on varg.ai: find-references

  2. 02

    Moodboard

    What must every frame look like, in numbers?

    Five palette colours measured from the reference plus one accent, light and framing per shot, type sizes. Five test stills on two image models, each checked against the palette

    Skill on varg.ai: video-moodboard

  3. 03

    Sound

    Who speaks, how fast, and where does the music stop?

    Six stock voices auditioned and three picked, 14 lines recorded, 4 re-recorded because the voice model rushed short lines. Two music cues, eight effects and a room tone. The picture is cut to this clock

    Skill on varg.ai: video-sound

  4. 04

    Script

    What is said, in how many seconds, and is each claim allowed?

    14 lines, 71 words. Two jokes come from replies that already had 955 and 684 likes; two lines are the reference's own narration. A facts file lists what may be said

    Skill on varg.ai: video-script

  5. 05

    Characters

    How do two real people appear without a generated face?

    Heads cut from licensed photos with a face mesh; the jaw drops in a slot like a marionette. Bodies and props generated from words on one sheet for $0.06

    Skill on varg.ai: character-passport

  6. 06

    Blockout

    Where is everyone, and where is the camera, on every frame?

    The whole film staged in three.js in metres and seconds, driven by the sound clock. Five rounds. One grey guide clip for the tracking shot

    Skill on varg.ai: code-to-video-previz

  7. 07

    Video generation

    Which shots need an AI video model, and which are better as code?

    Two shots went to Seedance 2.5 at 480p ($0.52 and $0.62, one take each). Four shots with faces, text and exact timing went to code

    Skill on varg.ai: video-animate

  8. 08

    Edit

    How do the pieces become one film that can be re-rendered?

    A HyperFrames project: photo puppets whose jaws follow the voice's loudness, captions, labels, the end card. Rendered eight times

    Skill on varg.ai: edit-footage-with-code

  9. 09

    AI judge

    What is wrong with it, measured, by someone who did not make it?

    Three separate agents measured the film. Round 1 found 2 major and 4 minor defects; round 3 found none on 13 checks

    Skill on varg.ai: media-judge

  10. 10

    GitHub

    Can the project be restored on another machine?

    In the private working repo: text in git, media in Releases, and a fresh copy compared byte for byte (149 text files and 408 media files matched). The repo is a cleaned copy of it

    Skill on varg.ai: video-project-github

How were the real people handled?

The film shows two real, named people, so the agent wrote rules before making anything (refs/never.md) and the judge checked them at the end.

  • Photos, not generated faces. Both heads are freely licensed photos from Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 4.0, CC BY 2.0, public domain), edited by code only: cut out, the jaw separated so it can move, a band of teeth drawn behind it, a darkened copy for the unlit shots. No generative model was given a photo of either man, and nothing generated, redrew or lip-synced a face. Two local models that do not generate touched the photos: one removed the background, one found the lips. Credits: CREDITS.md.
  • Bodies from words. The paper-doll bodies, the jacket and the card were generated from a text prompt that names nobody (cast/sheets/prompt.md).
  • Stock voices. Three library voices picked for pitch and pace. Nothing was cloned and none is meant to sound like either man. Nobody has checked that by ear.
  • A label on every frame. PARODY sits in the same corner from the first frame to the last (measured: 980 of 980 frames), and the end card says nobody said this.
  • It cannot pass for footage. White cut edges, a head too big for its body, a flat photo over a soft set. The judge's check "would a frame pass as real with the label cropped off" came back no.
  • It does not say what happened. The request's "model incident" appears as the opening title ("Nvidia's keynote opener, after the model incident") and as the word "model" in three lines of dialogue. The film does not say what the story was, this project did not verify it and repeats none of it, and nobody else is shown or named. The other jokes are about product habits: it folds, the cable is sold separately, it is a subscription now.

This is what this project did, not legal advice. Likeness and parody rules differ by country and by platform, and nobody checked them here.

Make your own

  1. Install the ten skills in your agent (Claude Code, Codex, Grok, Cursor, Gemini CLI and others). This needs Node.js for npx; the blockout, edit and renders also need ffmpeg, Python 3 and Bun:

    npx skills add vargHQ/skills --skill find-references --skill video-moodboard --skill video-sound \
  --skill video-script --skill character-passport --skill code-to-video-previz --skill video-animate \
  --skill edit-footage-with-code --skill media-judge --skill video-project-github

  2. Connect the models: add the varg MCP server https://mcp.varg.ai/mcp (sign in with OAuth) or set VARG_API_KEY from varg.ai. One key covers Seedance, Kling, Veo, GPT Image, Nano Banana and ElevenLabs. A new account starts with free credits, enough for stills but not for a film.

  3. Find the photos yourself or let the agent search: Wikimedia Commons lists the licence of every photo. This project took only CC BY and public-domain photos and kept their credits.

  4. Paste a request. The one below is this project's request with the blanks left for you (PROMPT.md has it with notes):

    Make a funny 480p parody video: <who> takes over from <who> because of <what happened>.
Mostly just take another funny video and adjust it.
Use their real, freely licensed photos as paper cut-outs. Never give a photo of a real
person to an image or video model, never clone a voice, and keep a PARODY label in every frame.
Work in ten steps with the varg skills: find-references, video-moodboard, video-sound,
video-script, character-passport, code-to-video-previz, video-animate, edit-footage-with-code,
media-judge, video-project-github.
Use https://github.com/SecurityQQ/ai-parody-video-claude-code as the worked example.
Show me a price estimate before you spend anything.

Rebuild the film

The edit is text, so the film can be rendered again or changed line by line. You need Python 3.9+ with Pillow, ffmpeg, Bun and the GitHub CLI.

git clone https://github.com/SecurityQQ/ai-parody-video-claude-code && cd ai-parody-video-claude-code
gh release download media-v1 -p media.zip && unzip -o media.zip      # photos, plates, voices, music, the two AI shots (49 MB)
git clone https://github.com/vargHQ/skills ../varg-skills              # the sound mixer and the judge live in the skills

python3 tools/build_sound.py                                           # voice lines onto the clock -> sound/cues.json, beats.json
python3 ../varg-skills/video-sound/scripts/sound.py mix sound/cues.json -o sound/mix.wav
python3 tools/build_edit.py                                            # assets + HyperFrames scenes -> edit/
cd edit && bunx hyperframes@0.9.15 check --json
bunx hyperframes@0.9.15 render --fps 24 --quality looks --output ../renders/film.mp4 && cd ..
python3 tools/measure_film.py renders/film.mp4                         # size, label on every frame, loudness

To change a line, edit LINES in tools/build_sound.py and record the new take with any text-to-speech model; the cut follows the sound. Pinned versions: hyperframes 0.9.15, gsap 3.15.0, mediapipe 0.10.14 (only for cutting the heads), voices on eleven_v3.

FAQ

Can Claude Code make a video?

Yes. Here it wrote the script, generated the voices, music, images and two video shots through varg, animated the rest as HTML and GSAP in HyperFrames, rendered the MP4 with ffmpeg and had other agents judge the result. Claude Code does not generate pixels itself: it writes code and calls models. The skills tell it which to use for what.

How much does an AI video like this cost?

This one cost $2.12 in model calls for 40.8 seconds at 480p. The two AI video shots were $1.14 of that; everything coded was free to render. Higher resolutions and more AI shots cost more, so the agent asks for an estimate before each call.

Which AI models were used?

Seedance 2.5 for two video shots, GPT Image 2.6 and Nano Banana 2.1 for background plates and the paper-doll sheet, ElevenLabs v3 for voices, ElevenLabs Music v2.5 for two cues and ElevenLabs Sound Effects v3 for eight effects and a room tone, all through varg. A video-understanding model (varg analyze_video) watched the reference clips. Whisper, a background-removal model and a face mesh model ran locally, for checks and for cutting the heads.

How do you make a parody of a real person with AI without making a deepfake?

This project kept the faces as licensed photos edited only by code (cut out, with a moving jaw), animated them as paper cut-outs, used stock voices, and kept a PARODY label on every frame. No generative model made, edited or lip-synced a real face. The exact rules are in refs/never.md; the law where you live and the rules of the platform you post on are yours to check.

How does the mouth move if no model animates the face?

Code cuts the lower jaw out of the photo along the lips, found with a face mesh, and slides it down in a slot, like a marionette. How far it drops follows the loudness of that character's voice line, frame by frame, held for two frames at a time. See tools/head_cut.py and tools/build_edit.py.

Why were only two shots made with an AI video model?

The agent's rule: exact things go to code, and a model gets only what nothing exact depends on. Faces from photos, on-screen text and timing to a spoken word are exact, so four shots are code. A wide shot of a walker and a tracking shot of sneakers have no face, no text and no word to hit, so they went to Seedance 2.5. The table is in shots/shots.md.

Why 480p?

The request asked for it. It also keeps AI video cheap, and since the film is finished at 480p there was no upscale pass.

Can I reuse the code?

Yes, the code and text are MIT licensed. The photos keep their own licences and need their credits (CREDITS.md). To make a different parody, replace the photos, the lines and the plates; the tools do not depend on who is in the film.

Is any of this real?

No. Nobody shown said these lines and the film makes no claim about real events. It is a joke built on a week of jokes on X, and it says so on every frame.
